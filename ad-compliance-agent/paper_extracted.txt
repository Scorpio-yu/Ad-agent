Abstract—Advertisement compliance detection plays an 
important role in regulating online commercial content. Although 
pre-trained language models and knowledge-enhanced methods 
such as K-BERT have achieved strong performance in text 
classification, existing approaches usually rely on uniform 
knowledge injection and lack task-oriented discriminative 
guidance. In advertisement compliance detection, however, 
violation identification is often dominated by surface-level lexical 
cues, making effective decision boundary modeling particularly 
important. To address this issue, we propose a Class-Aware 
Prompted K-BERT framework that incorporates category-
specific soft prompts into the K-BERT architecture. The proposed 
method guides the model to interpret advertisement texts from a 
compliance-aware perspective and enhances category-sensitive 
representation learning. In addition, we redesign the visible matrix 
to support effective interaction between prompt tokens and textual 
representations while preserving the hierarchical structure of 
knowledge injection. Experiments on an OCR-based e-commerce 
advertisement 
dataset 
show 
that 
the 
proposed 
method 
outperforms both BERT and K-BERT. The ablation study further 
indicates that direct knowledge injection does not always 
guarantee performance gains, while the proposed class-aware 
prompt mechanism can effectively improve advertisement 
compliance detection.  
Keywords—advertisement compliance detection; K-BERT; class-
aware prompt learning; knowledge-enhanced language model; e-
commerce advertising 
I. 
 INTRODUCTION  
The rapid expansion of e-commerce platforms has 
fundamentally transformed the landscape of commercial 
promotion. Online advertisements have become a primary 
channel for marketing communication, influencing purchasing 
decisions at an unprecedented scale. However, this growth has 
also led to a proliferation of non-compliant advertising content, 
including exaggerated claims, unverifiable efficacy statements, 
and misleading guarantees. Such content not only undermines 
consumer trust but also poses significant challenges for 
regulatory enforcement. Recent studies have explored fact-
checking 
for 
online 
advertisement 
posts[1], 
regulatory 
compliance analysis in social media influencer marketing[2], and 
sensitive content classification in broader moderation settings[3]. 
Consequently, automated advertisement compliance detection 
has emerged as an important task in intelligent content 
moderation systems. 
Recent advances in pre-trained language models (PLMs) 
have significantly improved the performance of various text 
classification tasks. Models such as BERT[4] leverage large-scale 
pre-training to capture contextualized semantic representations, 
enabling strong downstream performance. To further enhance 
semantic understanding, knowledge-enhanced models, most 
notably K-BERT[5], incorporate structured knowledge graphs 
into the encoding process. By injecting external knowledge into 
the input sequence and regulating attention through a visible 
matrix, K-BERT enriches contextual representations and 
improves reasoning ability in tasks involving implicit semantic 
relations. 
Despite these advantages, the assumption that knowledge 
injection universally benefits classification performance 
deserves 
reconsideration 
in 
domain-specific 
scenarios. 
Advertisement compliance detection differs from many 
conventional language understanding tasks. In practice, 
violation identification is often driven by explicit surface-level 
lexical cues, such as absolute expressions (“best”, “guaranteed”), 
exaggerated promotional claims, or implied medical effects. 
These signals frequently serve as strong discriminative 
indicators that can be captured directly from the original text. In 
such cases, additional knowledge may not necessarily improve 
classification performance and may even introduce redundant 
semantic information, especially when the external knowledge 
is not fully aligned with the target compliance scenario [1], [2]. 
Motivated by this observation, we propose a Class-Aware 
Prompted K-BERT framework for advertisement compliance 
detection. Instead of modifying the encoder architecture, the 
proposed method introduces label-specific soft prompt 


embeddings to guide the model toward compliance-oriented 
representation learning. By associating different prompts with 
different class hypotheses, the model interprets the same 
advertisement text from multiple decision perspectives, 
effectively reshaping the classification space. Our design is 
inspired by recent advances in prompt-based adaptation and 
prompt optimization[6]- [8]. In addition, we extend the visible 
matrix mechanism to ensure coherent interaction between 
prompt tokens and textual representations while maintaining the 
hierarchical structure of knowledge injection. 
Extensive experiments on an OCR-based e-commerce 
advertisement dataset demonstrate that the proposed framework 
consistently outperforms the original K-BERT implementation. 
Interestingly, strong performance is preserved even without 
knowledge graph injection, highlighting the dominant role of 
discriminative prompt guidance in surface-trigger-dominated 
compliance scenarios. These findings provide new insights into 
the interaction between prompt learning[6]-[8] and knowledge-
enhanced architectures[5]. 
II. 
RELATED WORK 
A. Advertisement Compliance Detection  
Early studies on advertisement compliance detection mainly 
relied on rule-based filtering and manually designed keyword 
lists. Although such approaches can capture explicit violation 
patterns, they often lack robustness when dealing with diverse 
linguistic expressions. With the development of deep learning, 
neural text classification models based on CNNs, RNNs, and 
transformer architectures have been widely adopted for content 
moderation tasks. In particular, pre-trained language models 
(PLMs) such as BERT[4] have significantly improved 
performance by leveraging large-scale contextual pre-training. 
Recent studies have also explored online advertisement fact-
checking, regulatory compliance analysis, and sensitive content 
moderation in related settings[1]-[3]. However, most existing 
methods treat compliance detection as a standard classification 
problem 
and 
rarely 
consider 
task-specific 
modeling 
characteristics. 
B. Knowledge-Enhanced Language Models 
To enhance semantic representation, knowledge-enhanced 
models incorporate structured knowledge graphs into language 
models. K-BERT[5] extends BERT by injecting knowledge 
triples into the input sequence and controlling attention through 
a visible matrix mechanism. Such methods have shown 
advantages in reasoning and relation extraction tasks. 
Nevertheless, existing knowledge injection strategies typically 
apply external knowledge uniformly to all inputs, without 
considering whether knowledge augmentation is necessary for 
specific classification scenarios. 
C. Prompt Learning in Pre-trained Language Models 
Prompt learning[6] has recently emerged as an effective 
paradigm for adapting pre-trained language models to 
downstream tasks. Soft prompt tuning introduces learnable 
continuous embeddings into the input sequence, enabling 
efficient task adaptation with limited additional parameters. 
Prompt mechanisms have been shown to reshape decision 
boundaries by guiding the model toward task-relevant features. 
However, the integration of class-aware prompt design with 
knowledge-enhanced architectures remains underexplored, 
particularly in domain-specific compliance detection tasks. 
III. 
METHOD 
A. Problem Definition 
Given an advertisement text 𝑋= {𝑥1, 𝑥2, … , 𝑥𝑛}, the task of 
advertisement compliance detection aims to predict its 
compliance label 𝑦∈{0,1}, where 0 denotes compliant content 
and 1 denotes non-compliant content. The objective is to learn a 
classification function: 
𝑓: 𝑋→𝑦
(1) 
Unlike general text classification tasks that rely on implicit 
semantic reasoning, compliance detection in advertising 
scenarios is often dominated by explicit surface-level lexical 
patterns, such as exaggerated claims and absolute expressions. 
These features act as strong discriminative signals, suggesting 
that modeling the decision boundary effectively may be more 
critical than enriching semantic representations through external 
knowledge. 
B. Revisiting K-BERT 
K-BERT extends the standard BERT architecture by 
injecting knowledge graph triples into the input sequence. For 
each token associated with an entity, relevant knowledge triples 
are expanded into a tree-like structure. The augmented input can 
be represented as:  
𝐻= [[𝐶𝐿𝑆], 𝑋, 𝐾]
(2) 
 
where 𝐾 denotes the knowledge-injected tokens. 
To prevent semantic drift introduced by knowledge 
expansion, K-BERT employs a visible matrix 𝑀to restrict 
attention propagation. Specifically: 
• 
Original text tokens attend to each other globally. 
• 
Knowledge tokens attend only to their anchor tokens 
and hierarchical paths. 
• 
Cross-branch attention is constrained. 
Although 
this 
mechanism 
enhances 
contextual 
representation, the knowledge injection process is uniformly 
applied across all samples, implicitly assuming that additional 
knowledge consistently benefits classification performance. 
Based on the original K-BERT backbone, our method introduces 
class-aware prompt embeddings and extends the visible matrix 
mechanism to better support compliance-oriented representation 
learning. The overall architecture of the proposed framework is 
shown in Fig. 1.

Inspired by recent advances in prompt learning and prompt 
optimization[6]-[8], 
we

E. Visible Matrix Extension 
To preserve the structural constraints of K-BERT while 
enabling prompt interaction, we extend the visible matrix 𝑀 as

1. 
Prompt tokens are globally visible to [CLS] and

2.

3. 
Prompt tokens do not directly attend to lower-level 
knowledge nodes.  
This design ensures that prompt embeddings function as 
global discriminative controllers without disrupting hierarchical 
knowledge structure.

IV. 
EXPERIMENTS 
A. Dataset and Experimental Settings 
To evaluate the proposed method, experiments are 
conducted on an OCR-based e-commerce advertisement dataset. 
The dataset is constructed by collecting advertisement images 
from e-commerce platforms and extracting textual content 
through optical character recognition (OCR). It contains 
approximately 12.4k samples, with about 7.8k labeled as 0 and 
about 4.6k labeled as 1, where label 1 denotes non-compliant 
advertisements. The violations mainly involve exaggerated 
claims, unverifiable efficacy descriptions, and misleading 
guarantees. The dataset is divided into training, development, 
and test sets with a ratio of 80%/10%/10%. For fair comparison, 
all models were trained and evaluated under the same 
experimental settings. Specifically, the maximum sequence 
length was set to 256, the batch size was set to 32, the learning 
rate was set to 2e-5, and the number of training epochs was set 
to 10. The encoder type was set to BERT, and the first-token 
pooling strategy was adopted for sentence representation. In 
addition, the random seed was fixed at 7 to improve 
reproducibility. 
B. Compared Methods  
To verify the contribution of each component, we compared 
the following three models: 
BERT: a vanilla BERT-based text classification model 
without knowledge injection or prompt enhancement. It serves 
as the basic pretrained language model baseline. 
K-BERT: extends BERT by injecting structured knowledge 
graphs into the input sequence and regulating attention through 
a visible matrix mechanism. 
Class-Aware Prompted K-BERT: the proposed method, 
which extends K-BERT by introducing class-aware soft prompts. 
Specifically, 
class-dependent 
prompt 
embeddings 
are 
incorporated into the input representation so that the model can 
better capture category-sensitive semantic patterns for 
advertisement compliance detection.

D. Ablation Study 
To further analyze the effectiveness of different 
components, we perform an ablation study with three variants: 
K-BERT w/o KG, K-BERT, and Class-Aware Prompted K-
BERT. The results are reported in Table II. 
Interestingly, K-BERT w/o KG performs slightly better 
than the original K-BERT, suggesting that direct knowledge 
injection does not always lead to better performance. One 
possible reason is that the injected knowledge may contain 
noisy or task-irrelevant information, which interferes with the 
original textual semantics. In this study, the domain-specific 
knowledge graph was mainly constructed from advertising 
regulations. Candidate triples were manually extracted from 
legal provisions and compliance-related rules, such as “cure 
completely”–belongs to–“absolute expression”, and further 
reviewed by relevant experts to ensure legal validity and 
compliance relevance. Although this process improves 
knowledge quality, the current graph may still have limited 
coverage and adaptability, which may partly explain the noise 
introduced by direct knowledge injection. 
The proposed Class-Aware Prompted K-BERT achieves 
the best results on all metrics, demonstrating that the class-
aware prompt mechanism can effectively enhance category-
sensitive representation learning and improve the utilization of 
knowledge-enhanced features. These results confirm that the 
proposed design provides a more effective framework for 
advertisement compliance detection.

V. 
CONCLUSIONS 
In this paper, we proposed a Class-Aware Prompted K-
BERT framework for advertisement compliance detection. By 
introducing class-aware prompt learning into K-BERT, the 
proposed method enhances category-sensitive representation 
learning and improves the discrimination between compliant 
and non-compliant advertisements. 
Experimental results show that the proposed method 
outperforms both BERT and K-BERT. The ablation study 
further indicates that direct knowledge injection does not 
always guarantee performance gains, while the proposed class-
aware prompt mechanism effectively improves classification 
performance. In future work, we will explore higher-quality 
advertisement knowledge graphs and more effective prompt 
learning strategies.
